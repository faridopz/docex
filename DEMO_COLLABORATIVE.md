# Collaborative Demo Framework — Learn From Them, Not At Them

**The Shift:** Don't present DOCex. Present a framework. Listen to how NEEM actually works. Build the solution together in the room.

---

## The Opening (2 min) — Set the Frame

Not: *"Here's what DOCex does."*

Rather:

> "I built a framework that handles the core finance workflow — receipts, approvals, audit trails. But every org is different. What I want to do today is show you what's possible, then dig into how YOU actually work so we can customize it for your specific pain points."

This does three things:
1. **Positions you as a builder, not a vendor** ("I built a framework")
2. **Signals partnership** ("customize it for your…")
3. **Sets up listening mode** ("dig into how YOU work")

---

## Segment 1: The Framework (4 min) — Show, Then Ask

**Show the field receipt screen.**

> "Here's how we handle field receipts. Fieldworkers photograph or upload them. The system reads what it can — vendor, amount, date — via OCR. Missing info gets flagged, but we don't guess. Does this look like how you'd want receipts to flow?"

**Stop. Listen to their answer.** They'll tell you:
- Do we use WhatsApp today? (Signal: offline capture is urgent)
- Do we lose photos? (Signal: need durability / audit trail)
- What info is missing most? (Signal: what to flag first)
- Is OCR good enough or do we need manual entry? (Signal: trade-off in build)

**Note their answer.** You're not selling, you're learning.

---

**Show the requisition screen.**

> "When someone raises a payment, policy checks run instantly — not days later. Thresholds, duplicate detection, grant period, vendor block list. What rules matter most to NEEM?"

Listen for:
- Do you have procurement thresholds? (3-quote rules?)
- Do people resubmit the same invoice by accident?
- Do you have grant codes and periods to enforce?
- Who decides what can be bought?

**Write down every answer.** This is the config work.

---

## Segment 2: The Blocking Check (3 min) — Live Governance

**Show the blocked payment (REQ-0002).**

> "This one is charged to a grant that ended. Donor rules block it. But here's the key: an approver can't just override it. They have to tick the check, write WHY they're releasing it, and name the authority. That reason and authority stay on the record forever."

**Don't oversell.** Just show it. Then ask:

> "When's the last time someone released an exception to policy? How was that recorded? Where does that note live?"

**Listen to the answer.** It'll probably be:
- "In an email somewhere" → Signal: audit trail is the win
- "Nowhere" → Signal: governance is the pain point
- "With a signature" → Signal: you're documenting what they're already doing

---

**Then ask the harder question:**

> "If we don't let someone override without a written reason and named authority, does that break your process? Or does it fix it?"

**This is diagnostic.** Their answer tells you if DOCex solves their real problem or if you're solving a problem they don't have.

---

## Segment 3: The Audit View (2 min) — The Close

**Show the audit dashboard.**

> "This is what your auditor opens. Every payment, every check, every override with its reason and who released it. The verdict: audit-ready or N unexplained exceptions. You fix them before the auditor arrives, not after."

**Don't explain it. Let them ask.**

If they don't ask: *"What usually happens when your auditor comes? What do they ask for first?"*

Their answer is the ROI story. Write it down.

---

## Segment 4: The Collaborative Close (3 min)

**Stop demoing. Start building.**

> "Here's what I hear: [reflect back their three biggest pain points]. What I'm thinking is: we could tackle [the one they said matters most] first, in the next 4 weeks. We'd configure YOUR grant codes, YOUR approval chain, YOUR required documents. Then you'd run it live with your team for a week, and we'd iterate based on what breaks."

**This is not a pitch.** This is a project kickoff in miniature.

> "Does that feel like the right approach? Or is there something else we should tackle first?"

**Then stop talking.** Let them answer.

---

## Discovery Questions (Baked In, Not Listed)

Don't hand them a question sheet. Weave these into the demo:

**Early (Segment 1):**
- "How do receipts move today?" ← Already asked during field receipts discussion
- "What info is most often missing?" ← Same conversation

**Middle (Segment 2):**
- "What rules matter most to NEEM?" ← Asked when showing requisitions
- "When's the last time someone released an exception?" ← Asked when showing override

**Later (Segment 3):**
- "What do auditors ask for first?" ← Asked at the audit view

**Closing (Segment 4):**
- "What's the one thing we should tackle first?" ← The real closing question

You're asking the same discovery questions, but **in context**, not as a list. This feels like a conversation, not an interrogation.

---

## The Listening Posture

**You're not:**
- Defending features not built
- Explaining why we do things differently
- Selling them on the vision

**You are:**
- Curious about how they actually work
- Taking notes on what matters to them
- Identifying which gap to solve first
- Building trust that you listen before you build

**Key phrases:**
- "Tell me more about that."
- "Has that caused problems?"
- "How do you know that happened?"
- "What would better look like?"
- "Let's dig into that in the next phase."

---

## If They Poke at Gaps

**They ask:** "Can it do X?" (where X is not built)

**Don't say:** "We haven't built that yet." (Defensive)

**Say:** *"Not yet. Is that blocking you from trying this, or is it nice-to-have?"*

If it's blocking, they just told you what to build first. Write it down.

If it's nice-to-have, say: *"Got it. Let's tackle the bigger problem first, then come back to this."*

---

## The Demo Isn't the Sale

**End state:** You leave with:
- ✅ What their three biggest pain points are
- ✅ Which one they want solved first
- ✅ Their org structure (departments, approvals, grant codes)
- ✅ A one-page project plan for the first 4 weeks

**You don't need:** A signed contract, a handshake, a "yes."

**You need:** A clear problem you can solve, and their willingness to let you try.

---

## After Tomorrow

Send them:

> "Here's what I heard: [pain point 1], [pain point 2], [pain point 3]. Based on that, here's what I'd build in the first month: [specific feature]. It would look like [one sentence]. You'd be able to [the value]. Thoughts?"

**That's the close.** You're not asking them to buy. You're asking them to react to your solution.

---

## The Tone (Throughout)

✅ Curious  
✅ Specific  
✅ Taking notes (actually write things down)  
✅ Asking follow-ups  
✅ Calm (no pressure to decide)

❌ Pitching  
❌ Defending  
❌ Overselling  
❌ Talking more than listening

---

## One Last Thing

The best demo happens when they forget you're demoing.

You're not showing them DOCex. You're thinking out loud *with them* about how to solve their problem. The software is just the vehicle for that conversation.

The moment they start imagining what it would look like in their org — that's when you know you've got them. That's when you stop demoing and start listening.
